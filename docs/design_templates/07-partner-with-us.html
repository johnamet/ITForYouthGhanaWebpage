Why partnership matters

Young people move through systems. So meaningful opportunity has to connect those systems too.

Schools, employers, communities, government, development organisations and technology companies each hold a different part of the wider environment around a learner. ITFYG's partnership work is strongest when those relationships create clearer pathways rather than isolated activities.

How partnership works

Start with the outcome, not the logo placement.

The partnership process should be clear enough for organisations to understand how conversations become actual work.

01
Find the shared goal

Clarify what both organisations want to make possible and for whom.

02
Design the relationship

Choose the programme, funding, expertise, reach or institutional contribution that fits.

03
Deliver with clear roles

Agree implementation, responsibilities, communication and accountability.

04
Learn from what happened

Use evidence, feedback and results to understand what should continue or change.

Partnership in practice

Tell the story of what the collaboration enabled.

In production, this should feature a verified partnership case study: the original challenge, the partner's role, ITFYG's role, what was delivered, who participated and what changed.

“The strongest partnership story is not that two organisations signed an agreement. It is that something useful became possible because they worked together.”
