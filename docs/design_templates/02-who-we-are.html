Who we are

Built around one belief: potential deserves a path.

IT For Youth Ghana exists to help young people turn curiosity, ability and ambition into practical digital capability—and then into something meaningful beyond the classroom.

Our story Meet the people
A youth-centred organisation shaped by learning, technology, community and the possibilities that follow.
Our story

We started with access. The work grew into opportunity.

ITFYG was built around a simple problem: too many young people have the interest and ability to participate in the digital world, but not enough have access to the tools, guidance, practice and relationships that make participation real.

The organisation's work therefore goes beyond exposure. Training, mentorship, community outreach, partnerships, practical projects and transition support are designed to connect learning with what comes next.

“The question is not whether young people are capable. The question is whether the path is visible, practical and within reach.”

The belief behind the work

Talent is everywhere. Opportunity should be too.

Our role is to help reduce the distance between potential and participation—through practical learning, confidence, community, partnerships and pathways into education, enterprise and work.

Our mission

Make digital opportunity more practical, human and reachable.

We create environments where young people can learn by doing, gain confidence through practice, and connect their skills to real possibilities beyond the programme itself.

Our vision

A future where young people can participate fully in the digital economy.

Not only as consumers of technology, but as builders, creators, professionals, entrepreneurs and leaders.

How we work

Principles that keep the work grounded.

The organisation is strongest when the experience remains practical, inclusive and connected to the realities young people face. These principles should shape programme design, partnerships and the way ITFYG grows.

01

Practical before performative.

Learning should lead to something a young person can actually do, build, show or use.

02

Access must lead somewhere.

Exposure matters, but stronger programmes also create progression, mentorship and visible next steps.

03

Context matters.

Young people, schools, communities and organisations do not all need the same intervention.

04

Partnership multiplies reach.

Meaningful collaboration helps programmes travel farther, improve faster and connect learners to wider opportunity.
